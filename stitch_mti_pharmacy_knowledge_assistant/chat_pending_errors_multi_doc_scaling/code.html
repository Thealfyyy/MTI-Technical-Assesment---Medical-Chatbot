help_outline Inquiry: Protocol guidelines for surgical prophylaxis in colorectal resections?
Dr. N. Al-Mansoor · General Surgery

Antimicrobial prophylaxis in elective colorectal procedures is critical for mitigating post-operative Surgical Site Infections (SSIs). Established international consensus mandates targeted coverage against both aerobic enteric Gram-negative bacilli and anaerobic organisms, predominantly Bacteroides fragilis. Standard parenteral administration should consist of Cefoxitin or Cefotetan alone, or alternatively, a first-line cephalosporin such as Cefazolin combined strictly with Metronidazole.

Critical pharmacokinetic execution requires infusion within 60 minutes prior to surgical incision to ensure optimal peak tissue concentrations at initial epidermal disruption. For procedures exceeding two drug half-lives (typically greater than 3 to 4 hours for standard Cefazolin regimens), intraoperative redosing is mandatory to preserve minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs). Excess blood loss greater than 1,500 mL warrants immediate supplemental redosing irrespective of procedural elapsed time.

Prolongation of antimicrobial regimens beyond the operative period exhibits zero clinical efficacy in reducing SSI occurrences while exponentially escalating the risk of acute nephrotoxicity, secondary resistance acquisition, and Clostridioides difficile colitis. Discontinuation at surgical closure represents standard compliance across both the ASHP and WHO SSI prevention frameworks.
